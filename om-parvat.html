Om Parvat

Miraculous 6,191m peak where natural snow accumulation permanently forms the sacred Sanskrit syllable ॐ — viewed from Nabidhang at 4,246m in Pithoragarh district.

What is Om Parvat?

Om Parvat is one of the most extraordinary natural wonders in the Himalayas — a 6,191-metre sacred mountain peak in the Vyas Valley of Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand, where natural snow permanently accumulates on the black rock face in the precise shape of the sacred Sanskrit syllable ॐ (Om). This divine formation is entirely natural, formed by glacial and seasonal snow that fills a specific pattern of rock crevices on the mountain face, creating a permanent and unmistakable OM symbol visible to the naked eye from the viewpoint at Nabidhang (4,246m).

Om Parvat is among the most emotionally and spiritually powerful sights in Indian pilgrimage tradition. Standing at Nabidhang and beholding the glowing white OM on the dark Himalayan rock — in the silence of the high-altitude valley — is an experience that devotees describe as transcendental and deeply moving. The peak lies near the trijunction of India, Nepal, and Tibet, adding to its cosmic mystique.

History & Spiritual Significance

Om Parvat has been sacred to Hindus for millennia, though it gained widespread modern recognition in the late 20th century when photographs of the natural OM formation on its face were first widely circulated. The mountain stands as a testament to what devotees across all Hindu traditions regard as an undeniable divine signature — the sacred syllable OM, the primordial sound of creation in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain cosmology, permanently inscribed by nature on the face of a Himalayan peak.

The viewpoint at Nabidhang (4,246m) is where all Adi Kailash yatris stop for the Om Parvat darshan. The name Nabidhang refers to a high-altitude flat meadow from which the peak is clearly visible on clear days. Pilgrims perform a brief puja and often sit in silent meditation upon beholding the OM formation. The moment of first sighting is traditionally accompanied by ringing of bells and singing of bhajans by the group.

Om Parvat is located near the Kalapani trijunction — the disputed territorial tri-point of India, Nepal, and China (Tibet). The surrounding area holds immense geo-strategic significance and is patrolled by the ITBP (Indo-Tibetan Border Police). The Inner Line Permit (ILP) required for entry to this zone is obtained through Rudraansh Yatra as part of all packages.

How to Reach

  1. From Gunji/Nabi Homestay Base: On Day 4 of the yatra, drive from the Nabi/Gunji homestay further up the Vyas Valley towards the Nabidhang meadow and ITBP post.
  2. Nabidhang Viewpoint (4,246m): This is the designated Om Parvat viewpoint. Pilgrims alight from the vehicle and walk 50–100 metres to the viewing area. The OM on the peak face is clearly visible on clear mornings.
  3. Early Morning Recommended: Clouds often build up after 10:00–11:00 AM at this altitude. Arriving at Nabidhang by 7:00–9:00 AM maximises chances of a clear darshan.
  4. Via Rudraansh Yatra Package: Om Parvat darshan is integrated into the 4-day itinerary of all Adi Kailash packages (on Day 3 or Day 4 depending on the package). No separate booking needed.

Best Time to Visit

Om Parvat darshan is best from May to mid-June and September to October. Post-monsoon October months typically offer the clearest skies, giving the most vivid view of the OM snow formation on the dark rock face. Monsoon season (July–August) brings heavy cloud cover and restricted visibility. The route is completely closed in winter due to snowfall blocking the Chialekh Pass from the valley side.
