1. Reopening Milestones: How Permits Resumed on September 20

For several weeks, devotees across India watched the Kumaon skies anxiously. Late monsoon rainfall had triggered debris flows along the Lipulekh highway between Tawaghat and Najang. In an abundance of caution, the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) of Dharchula temporarily paused physical entries, updating the expected reopening from September 15 to September 20.

Right on schedule on September 20, 2026, the SDM administrative desk issued formal clearances. Border checkposts manned by the Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP 7th & 14th Battalions) at Tawaghat, Garbyang, and Gunji unlocked their barrier gates for civilian pilgrim convoys. The Border Roads Organisation (BRO Project Hirak) deployed specialized bulldozers and JCBs along vulnerable bends to ensure total stability.

2. Inside the 1st Batch: 25 Yatris, 5 Days, 100% Success

Rudraansh Yatra’s first autumn batch was a diverse group of 25 pilgrims representing different parts of India — including families from Ahmedabad, senior devotees from Pune, spiritual seekers from Bangalore and Delhi NCR. Because our ground operations are physically based in Pithoragarh and Dharchula, our team accompanied the batch at every checkpoint.

Day 1 • Sept 20, 2026 Dharchula to Nabi / Gunji (3,200 m)

Permit Verification & Scenic Ascent along Kali River

At 7:00 AM, our fleet of customized 4x4 Boleros gathered at Dharchula. Within 45 minutes, all 25 Inner Line Permits and medical slips were verified at Tawaghat ITBP checkpost. Navigating via Lakhanpur, Najang, Bundi, and the historic sinking village of Garbyang, the batch ascended through the pine-scented Chhialekh pass, reaching the picturesque village of Nabi by late afternoon for hot herbal tea and warm hospitality in traditional homestays.

Day 2 • Sept 21, 2026 Kalapani, Kali Mata Mandir & Vyas Gufa

Acclimatization, Sacred Springs & Cultural Immersion

To ensure high-altitude acclimatization, yatris visited the holy Kali Mata Temple at Kalapani — the mythical origin of the Mahakali River — and took darshan of the ancient Vyas Gufa where Maharishi Ved Vyas meditated. Oximeter readings were checked twice daily (pulse & SpO2 > 90% across all 25 pilgrims), accompanied by nutritious Kumaoni millet meals.

Day 3 • Sept 22, 2026 Nabi to Nabhidhang • Om Parvat Darshan (13,940 ft)

The Miraculous Snow-Etched “ॐ” under Cloudless Skies

Departing Nabi at sunrise, the batch arrived at the Nabhidhang viewing point (4,250 m). With zero clouds and crisp golden morning light, the natural snow-etched “OM” symbol on Om Parvat was visible in breathtaking, razor-sharp detail. Yatris performed group chants of Om Namah Shivaya, capturing lifelong memories and emotional prayers before returning to Nabi.

Day 4 • Sept 23, 2026 Jolingkong, Parvati Sarovar & Mount Adi Kailash (14,760 ft)

Pinnacle Darshan: Parikrama, Temple Aarti & Gauri Kund

The most sacred day of the expedition. The convoy traversed the Kutti valley plateau to Jolingkong Base Camp (4,300 m). All 25 yatris walked the sacred path to Parvati Sarovar, offered sacred water to Lord Shiva at the lakeside temple, and marveled at the pyramid peak of Mount Adi Kailash towering directly overhead. A few energetic yatris took the steep walk to Gauri Kund.

Day 5 • Sept 24-25, 2026 Return Descent: Gunji to Dharchula & Pithoragarh

Safe Return, Gratitude Aarti & Zero Emergencies

With satisfied hearts and blessed souls, the batch descended smoothly back through the Vyas Valley to Dharchula and onwards to Pithoragarh. Every single yatri completed the pilgrimage in prime health, praising the warmth of Nabi homestays, the caution of our mountain drivers, and our dedicated oxygen backup support.

4. Autumn Weather & Temperature Profile (Sept – Oct 2026)

Autumn (late September through October) is widely acknowledged as the most pristine weather window of the entire year in the Upper Himalayas. The monsoon moisture has completely evacuated the atmosphere, leaving behind crisp, transparent azure skies without a trace of fog or haze.

  • Daytime Temperatures (Gunji / Nabi 3,200 m): 10°C to 15°C. Pleasant with warm radiant mountain sunshine. Light jackets or fleeces are sufficient during the day.
  • Nighttime Temperatures (Gunji / Nabi): 0°C to -3°C. Traditional homestays provide heavy quilts, hot water bottles, and heated rooms.
  • High Altitudes (Jolingkong / Om Parvat 4,300 m): 4°C to 8°C during peak noon, dropping near -5°C at dawn and night. Thermal innerwear, windproof gloves, and woolen beanies are compulsory.
  • Photography & Visibility: 10/10. Ideal for astrophotography (Milky Way viewing from Nabi) and crystal-clear telephoto shots of Mount Adi Kailash and Om Parvat.

5. Vital Advice for Yatris Planning October Batches

With permits flowing and the 1st batch setting a sparkling precedent, bookings for October departures are filling rapidly. Here are 4 essential ground rules to follow:

  1. Do Not Delay Permit Paperwork: The SDM office and ITBP issue a strictly controlled number of permits daily to prevent overcrowding in high-altitude homestays. Submit your Aadhaar copy, doctor fitness certificate, and police verification to your operator at least 7 to 10 days in advance.
  2. Opt for 4x4 Boleros Handled by Local Drivers: The terrain beyond Tawaghat requires drivers who know every curve, rock-fall frequency, and weather nuance. Rudraansh Yatra operates exclusively with veteran local drivers native to Dharchula and Pithoragarh.
  3. Follow Acclimatization Discipline: Hydrate well (3 to 4 liters of warm water daily), avoid rushing on the Parvati Sarovar steps, and carry a basic personal medical kit with Diamox (if prescribed by your doctor) and camphor tablets for inhalation.
  4. Remember the Pre-Winter Closing: The yatra season cannot run indefinitely. By late October or early November, the border villages begin their winter migration (Kooch) to Dharchula, and ITBP checkposts freeze permits for the winter. October is your last window until May 2027!

6. Frequently Asked Questions (Ground Reality & Permits)

Did Adi Kailash permits officially start from September 20, 2026?
Yes. The online Inner Line Permit (ILP) application portal and SDM Dharchula administrative office officially resumed permit issuance on September 20, 2026, following road restoration by BRO Project Hirak along the Tawaghat-Sobla-Gunji stretch.
Has the first batch completed darshan after the reopening?
Yes. Rudraansh Yatra's 1st Autumn 2026 batch comprising 25 pilgrims departed Dharchula on September 20 and successfully completed the complete circuit of Om Parvat, Parvati Sarovar, and Mount Adi Kailash by September 24-25, 2026, with a 100% completion rate.
Is the road safe for senior citizens and families right now?
Yes. Our first batch included senior pilgrims aged 58 to 68 who traveled comfortably in 4x4 Boleros. With gradual elevation gain, overnight stays at Nabi (3,110 m), and proper hydration, senior citizens experienced no acute mountain sickness (AMS). Portable oxygen cylinders and oximeters are carried throughout.
What documents are required to apply for the Inner Line Permit (ILP)?
You need: (1) Valid Govt Photo ID (Aadhaar Card preferred), (2) Police Verification Certificate / Character Certificate from your local police station or passport copy, (3) Medical Fitness Certificate signed by an MBBS doctor, and (4) 4 passport-size photographs. Rudraansh Yatra manages the complete submission with SDM Dharchula.
When will the 2026 yatra season close for winter?
Permit issuance typically closes around late October or the first week of November depending on winter snowfall in the high passes and the traditional seasonal migration (Kooch) of local Rung villagers down to Dharchula.
