The High-Altitude Challenge: Running at 3,200m–4,600m in Vyas Valley

Organized under the aegis of the Uttarakhand Tourism Development Board (UTDB) and the District Administration of Pithoragarh, the Adi Kailash Parikrama Run 2.0 is one of India's most physically demanding athletic events with over 1,100 runners from 26 states competing across 5 categories. Held in the shadow of sacred peaks near the Indo-Tibet-Nepal tri-junction, the flagship 70 KM Ultra Run started at Jyolingkong (4,600m / 15,000 ft) — the base of Adi Kailash — and finished at Gunji (3,200m), while the 21.1K, 10K, and 5K categories ran the Dharchula–Gunji corridor, the same route used by pilgrims on the Adi Kailash Yatra.

The event, felicitated by Uttarakhand Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami, is part of the Government of India's Vibrant Villages Programme, designed to promote border tourism and bring economic opportunities to the remote Rung tribal settlements. Athletes crossed the same rugged mountain terrain that yatris traverse to seek blessings of Adi Kailash (Chota Kailash) and Om Parvat. Learn about Om Parvat Darshan →

💡 Note on Yatra Route Logistics: To guarantee runner safety, medical response availability, and unimpeded movement on the narrow mountain roads of Vyas Valley, tourist Inner Line Permits (ILP) were temporarily paused by SDM Dharchula between October 19 and October 26, 2026. Regular pilgrim movement resumes smoothly from October 27 onwards. Read the complete ILP guide →

Vibrant Border Tourism & The Rung Community of Gunji

Beyond the competition, the Adi Kailash Parikrama Run plays a transformative role in highlighting the remote border villages of Uttarakhand under the Prime Minister's Vibrant Villages Programme. Village Gunji, situated at the confluence of the Kali and Kuti Yankti rivers at approximately 3,200 metres, transformed into an energetic sporting village for 24 hours. The same route connects pilgrims to Jolingkong Lake, the sacred campsite beside Adi Kailash, visible from the racecourse.

Athletes stayed in authentic Rung tribal homestays across Gunji, Nabi, and Napalchu, experiencing local culinary traditions like hot Madua rotis, Gahat dal, and herbal mountain teas that aided their recovery in sub-zero nighttime temperatures. These homestays are also available for Adi Kailash Yatra pilgrims — explore our Adi Kailash packages that include authentic Rung homestay accommodations.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Where can runners find their official timing certificates?

Official race certificates and timing split data can be accessed directly on the Runizen portal at runizen.com/events/adi-kailash-parikrama-run-2-0-2026-darchula-gunji by entering your bib number or registered runner name.

What elevation did the Parikrama Run take place at?

The event course in Gunji village, Vyas Valley is located at an altitude of approximately 3,200 metres (10,500 feet), with surrounding trail sections climbing higher toward Nabi and Rongkong.

When do regular yatra permits resume after the race?

General pilgrimage Inner Line Permits (ILP) officially resumed on October 27, 2026, allowing yatris to complete their autumn pilgrimage before the winter snowfall closure in early November.

Can runners stay in local homestays in Gunji?

Yes, traditional Rung homestays in Gunji, Nabi, and Napalchu host both runners and pilgrims. Pre-booking is mandatory through authorized local operators like Rudraansh Yatra due to limited capacity in the high-altitude border valley.

How do I plan the Adi Kailash Yatra 2027?

To plan the Adi Kailash Yatra 2027, you need an Inner Line Permit (ILP) from the SDM Dharchula office, transport in a 4x4 vehicle on the Dharchula–Gunji border road, and accommodation across the 6–7 day itinerary.
