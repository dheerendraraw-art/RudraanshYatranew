What is Om Parvat?

Om Parvat (also called Adi Kailash's companion peak) stands at 6,191 metres in the Kumaon Himalayas of Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand. The mountain gained its name from the remarkable natural phenomenon: snow permanently accumulates on the rocky face of the mountain in the shape of the Sanskrit syllable "OM" (ॐ).

The phenomenon is visible from a viewpoint at Nabidhang (4,246m) in Vyas Valley. The OM formation is caused by the unique geometry of the mountain's rock faces, which prevents snow from settling on specific sections while accumulating on others — creating a negative-space rendering of the sacred symbol.

For devout Hindus, Om Parvat is considered just as sacred as the more famous Mt. Kailash in Tibet. It is part of the Panch Kailash — the five sacred Kailash peaks in the Himalayas.

How to Reach Om Parvat Viewpoint (Nabidhang)

The Om Parvat viewpoint at Nabidhang is only accessible with a proper Inner Line Permit (ILP) and a 4x4 vehicle with an experienced border-route driver. Here is the step-by-step route:

  1. Reach Pithoragarh — by road from Kathgodam (6 hrs) or Delhi (12-14 hrs). Alternatively, fly to Pantnagar Airport (90 mins away).
  2. Drive to Dharchula (915m, ~2 hrs from Pithoragarh). ILP verification at ITBP checkpoint.
  3. Drive to Gunji / Nabi (3,200m, 4-5 hrs from Dharchula via Chialekh Pass). Overnight stay in Bhotiya homestay. Acclimatize.
  4. Drive to Nabidhang (4,246m, 1-2 hrs from Gunji) by 4x4. Om Parvat darshan from the viewpoint. Best viewed in early morning.

Is Om Parvat Accessible Without an Operator?

Technically possible, but practically very difficult. Here is what makes it challenging on your own:

  • Inner Line Permit: Requires co-ordination with the Dharchula SDM office. Applications rejected if any document is missing or submitted late.
  • 4x4 Vehicle: The Gunji-to-Nabidhang route requires a modified Bolero or similar 4x4 with an experienced driver who knows the border route checkpoints.
  • Accommodation: Vyas Valley has only tribal homestays — no hotels. Must be pre-arranged with local contacts.
  • Emergency: No mobile network in Vyas Valley. Medical evacuations require helicopter co-ordination through ITBP.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we trek to Om Parvat summit?

No. Om Parvat (6,191m) is not open for mountaineering by civilians. It is a restricted border peak. Darshan is only possible from the Nabidhang viewpoint at 4,246m by road (4x4 vehicle). No climbing permit is issued for this peak.

How far is Om Parvat from Pithoragarh?

The Nabidhang viewpoint (where Om Parvat is visible) is approximately 200 km from Pithoragarh by road — roughly 8-10 hours driving via Dharchula, Chialekh Pass, and Gunji.

Is photography allowed at Om Parvat viewpoint?

Yes, photography is permitted at the Nabidhang viewpoint. However, photography is strictly prohibited near ITBP checkpoints and military installations along the route. Our guide will advise on restricted zones during the yatra.
