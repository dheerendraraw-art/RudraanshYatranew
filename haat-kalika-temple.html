Maa Haat Kalika Temple, Gangolihat

Revered Mahakali Shakti shrine at 1,760 m, consecrated by Adi Shankaracharya & Isht Devi of the Indian Army's Kumaon Regiment. Open 5 AM – 9 PM · Entry Free.

What is Maa Haat Kalika Temple?

Maa Haat Kalika Temple (also written as Hat Kalika Mandir) is an ancient and powerful Mahakali Shaktipeeth located in Gangolihat town, Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand, at an altitude of 1,760 metres (5,774 ft) above sea level. Nestled amidst a dense canopy of deodar and chir pine forests, the shrine is dedicated to Goddess Mahakali — the primordial, protective, and transformative form of the Divine Mother.

According to regional tradition, Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya visited Gangolihat during his Himalayan pilgrimage in the 8th century CE. Perceiving the fierce manifestation of the Mother Goddess here, he installed a sacred Shakti Yantra and consecrated the temple, transforming her fierce energy into a benevolent, protective presence. Some structures in the temple complex date back to the 9th–11th centuries CE, attesting to the shrine's antiquity.

The Sacred Bond with the Kumaon Regiment

Maa Haat Kalika holds a unique and legendary bond with the Indian Armed Forces, specifically the renowned Kumaon Regiment. The regiment's battle cry is "Kalika Mata Ki Jai" (Victory to Mother Kalika), and the Goddess is worshipped as their Isht Devi (patron goddess).

Military lore states that during the 1971 Indo-Pak war and numerous frontier battles, soldiers had visions of the Mother Goddess guarding their positions. Battalions visit Gangolihat before deployment and return to present brass bells and offer thanksgiving pooja upon victorious return. The temple complex is adorned with thousands of brass bells gifted by brave soldiers across generations. The Kumaon Regiment has also constructed an Army Rest House for visiting military personnel.

Capt. Vikram Batra, Param Vir Chakra — The celebrated Kargil War hero (Shershaah) who said "Yeh dil maange more" was a devoted worshipper of Maa Haat Kalika. He is believed to have sought her blessings before joining his battalion. His deep faith in the Goddess remains an enduring part of the temple's living legend.

Spiritual Atmosphere & Sacred Rituals

The temple is built in traditional Pahadi Himalayan architecture with multi-tiered slate roofs, stone walls, and ornate woodcarvings. An eternal sacred fire (Akhand Dhuni) burns continuously within the temple premises. Pilgrims offer red Chunari, coconuts, marigold garlands, and ringing brass bells. The air is thick with the scent of dhoop and the sound of conch shells, especially during morning and evening aarti.

During Navratri (both Chaitra Navratri in March-April and Ashwin Navratri in October), vibrant fairs and extended rituals draw devotees from across Uttarakhand, Nepal, and North India. Dussehra is celebrated with special grandeur by the Kumaon Regiment, with military ceremonies conducted alongside traditional religious rites.

How to Reach Haat Kalika Temple

By Air: The nearest airport is Pantnagar Airport (PGH), approximately 240 km from Gangolihat. From Pantnagar, hire a taxi or take a bus via Almora or Pithoragarh.

By Train: The nearest railway stations are Kathgodam (KGM) (~190 km) and Tanakpur (~165 km). Both are well-connected to Delhi, Lucknow, and other major cities. Shared taxis to Pithoragarh/Gangolihat are available from both stations.

By Road: Gangolihat is 75 km from Pithoragarh town via NH-9. Private 4x4 vehicles (Bolero, Innova) are recommended for the mountain roads. Rudraansh Yatra's packages include native 4x4 transport from Pithoragarh, Kathgodam, and Delhi NCR.
