Maa Haat Kalika Temple, Gangolihat

Revered Mahakali Shakti shrine nestled inside deodar forests, consecrated by Adi Shankaracharya and patron deity of the Indian Army's Kumaon Regiment.

What is Maa Haat Kalika Temple?

Maa Haat Kalika Temple (also known as Hat Kalika Mandir) is an ancient and immensely powerful Shaktipeeth located in the historic town of Gangolihat in the Pithoragarh district of Uttarakhand. Nestled amidst towering deodar and chir pine forests, the shrine is dedicated to Goddess Mahakali in her protective, motherly form.

According to regional history, Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya visited Gangolihat during his travels across the Himalayas in the 8th century CE. Seeing the fiery and fierce manifestation of the Mother Goddess, he consecrated a sacred Shakti Yantra and installed it in the temple, transforming her fierce energy into a benevolent, protective presence for humanity.

The Sacred Bond with the Kumaon Regiment

Maa Haat Kalika holds a unique and legendary bond with the Indian Armed Forces, specifically the renowned Kumaon Regiment and Naga Regiment. The war cry of the Kumaon Regiment is "Kalika Mata Ki Jai" (Victory to Mother Kalika).

Military lore states that during the 1971 Indo-Pak war and numerous other frontier battles, soldiers saw mysterious visions of the Mother Goddess guarding their positions. To this day, battalions of the Kumaon Regiment visit Gangolihat before deployment to seek her blessings, and return to present brass bells and offer thanksgiving pooja upon their victorious return. The temple complex is adorned with thousands of bells gifted by brave soldiers.

Spiritual Atmosphere & Divine Rituals

The temple is constructed in traditional Himalayan Pahadi stone-and-timber architecture with multi-tiered slate roofs and ornate wood carvings. An eternal sacred fire (Akhand Dhuni) burns continuously in the temple premises. Pilgrims offer red Chunari, coconuts, flowers, and ringing brass bells. During Navratri (both Chaitra and Ashwin), vibrant fairs and devotional rituals take place, drawing devotees from all across Uttarakhand, Nepal, and north India.
