Quick Answer: What is Patal Bhuvaneshwar?

Open Daily · 7 AM – 6 PM

Patal Bhuvaneshwar is an ancient subterranean limestone cave temple dedicated to Lord Shiva (Bhuvaneshwar Mahadev), located at an elevation of 1,350 metres in Gangolihat tehsil of Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand. Pilgrims descend 90 feet (27 m) into the earth using heavy-duty steel chains through a narrow limestone tunnel. According to the Skanda Purana, the 160-metre-long cavern naturally preserves the stalactite and stalagmite rock formations representing 33 crore (330 million) Hindu deities.

What is Patal Bhuvaneshwar Cave Temple?

Patal Bhuvaneshwar (पाताल भुवनेश्वर) is one of India's most extraordinary, spiritually charged, and geologically mesmerizing subterranean shrines. Tucked away amidst fragrant pine and deodar forests 14 km from Gangolihat town in the Pithoragarh district of Uttarakhand, this ancient limestone cave descends vertically into the subterranean realm (Pātāla Loka).

Unlike man-made stone temples, the shrines inside Patal Bhuvaneshwar are entirely Svayambhu (self-manifested), sculpted over millions of years by the slow, sacred deposition of mineral-rich water dripping from limestone ceilings (karst speleothems). Revered as the subterranean abode of Lord Shiva, the cave is recognized by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) as an invaluable national monument and geological wonder.

Puranic Legend & Rediscovery by Adi Shankaracharya

The sacred significance of Patal Bhuvaneshwar is documented extensively in the Manas Khanda of the Skanda Purana. The text proclaims that visiting Patal Bhuvaneshwar bestows equal spiritual merit to performing pilgrimage across all four sacred Dhams—Badrinath, Kedarnath, Gangotri, and Yamunotri.

According to Vedic chronicles, the cave was first discovered in the Treta Yuga by King Rituparna of Ayodhya (an ancestor of Lord Rama). While chasing a wounded deer, Rituparna was guided deep into the forest by the celestial serpent Sheshnag, who escorted the king through the subterranean chambers and unveiled the living pantheon of 33 crore deities worshipping Lord Shiva.

In the Dvapara Yuga, the five Pandava brothers and Draupadi meditated in this cavern during their Himalayan exile before embarking on their final ascent toward Swargarohini. Following the Pandavas' departure, the divine entrance was sealed in stone.

In 1191 CE (Kali Yuga), the great philosopher-saint Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya was guided spiritually to the hidden portal during his voyage across Kumaon. He consecrated the subterranean sanctum, installed protective copper plates near the holy Shivling, and entrusted the sacred daily worship to the Bhandari Brahmin families of Bhuvneshwar village, whose descendants serve as authorized custodians to this day.

Inside the Cavern: The Divine Calcite Rock Formations

As you step off the final link of the steel descent chains onto the cavern floor, you enter a cool, ethereal subterranean cathedral illuminated by amber electric lamps. The cave ceiling and walls reveal naturally formed limestone sculptures:

Sheshnag's Hood & Spine

A massive, arching limestone ceiling sculpted like the seven-headed celestial serpent Sheshnag holding up the earth, accompanied by a long, segmented stalactite resembling the serpent's spinal column.

Ganesha's Head & Brahma Kamal

A smooth rock formation of Lord Ganesha's severed head, positioned directly beneath a 108-petaled calcite Brahma Kamal lotus that continuously drips single drops of mineral nectar onto the idol.

The Four Yuga Pillars

Four distinct stalagmites representing Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali Yuga. Legend holds that the Kali Yuga pillar grows a fraction of a millimetre annually; when it reaches the ceiling, a cosmic epoch resets.

Kamdhenu Cow Udders

Calcite stalactites shaped like the udders of Kamdhenu (the wish-fulfilling divine cow). Clear limestone water naturally drips from these formations directly over a consecrated Shiva Lingam.

Kalpavriksha (Wishing Tree)

A sprawling calcite structure on the cavern wall with intricate branch-like mineral draperies, revered by devotees as the mythological wish-granting celestial tree.

Char Dham Replicas

Three natural stone formations clustered together resembling the sacred shrines of Kedarnath, Badrinath, and the naturally frozen ice-lingam of holy Amarnath cave.

Shiva's Jata & Ganga Descent

Cascading mineral draperies depicting the wild, matted locks (Jata) of Mahadev holding back the surging torrents of the descending celestial river Ganga.

Swans of Brahma & Garuda Wings

Wing-like calcite projections jutting out from side crevices representing Lord Brahma's celestial Hamsa (swans) and Lord Vishnu's divine mount, Garuda.

The 80-Foot Chain Descent: Step-by-Step Guide

The journey into Patal Bhuvaneshwar begins at the surface temple courtyard. Pilgrims remove their shoes and descend into an angled rock fissure roughly 3 feet wide and 80 feet long, inclined at a 45-to-60-degree pitch.

Essential Descent Safety Protocols

While the descent is safe and undertaken by thousands of devotees each month, following proper body mechanics is essential for a smooth experience:

Descend Facing the Rock: Always walk backward facing the stone wall, keeping three points of contact.
Grip the Chains Firmly: Two parallel heavy-gauge stainless-steel chains provide solid hand support on both left and right sides.
Wear Thick Cotton Socks: Shoes are removed outside; thick socks provide friction and prevent slipping on smooth limestone.
Well-Ventilated Interior: Continuous oxygen airflow is maintained by natural rock vents, and the cave stays at a comfortable 18°C.

Who Can Safely Visit?

  • Suitable for: Adults, senior citizens with normal knee flexion, and children aged 7 and above with basic balance.
  • Not Recommended for: Individuals suffering from acute claustrophobia, severe asthma, heart ailments, third-trimester pregnancy, or advanced knee arthritis. Devotees unable to descend can offer prayers at the surface temple of Goddess Bhuvneshwari.

Best Time to Visit Patal Bhuvaneshwar

Patal Bhuvaneshwar is an all-weather pilgrimage destination that remains open 365 days a year. However, certain seasons offer optimal road and climatic conditions:

  • Autumn (September to November) — Best Season: Clear blue skies, crisp Himalayan air, panoramic views of the Panchachuli and Nanda Devi ranges, and ideal road conditions following the monsoon. Highly recommended for combining with the Adi Kailash Yatra.
  • Spring & Summer (March to June) — Ideal for Families: Pleasant weather with temperatures between 15°C and 25°C. Perfect for senior citizens and school holiday family excursions.
  • Winter (December to February) — Tranquil & Chilly: Quiet, unhurried cave darshans. Daytime temperatures average 10°C to 15°C, though nights are cold (2°C to 5°C). Heavy woollens are required.
  • Monsoon (July to August): The interior cavern remains completely dry and well-lit. However, travellers should stay updated on mountain road landslide clearances between Haldwani and Almora.
