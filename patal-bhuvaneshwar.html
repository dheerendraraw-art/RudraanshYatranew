Patal Bhuvaneshwar Cave Temple

Mystical 160m underground cavern descending 90 feet into the earth, enshrining the naturally sculpted stone formations of 33 crore deities and Lord Shiva.

What is Patal Bhuvaneshwar Cave Temple?

Patal Bhuvaneshwar is one of the most enigmatic, sacred, and geologically astounding underground cave shrines in India. Located 14 km from Gangolihat in the Pithoragarh district of Uttarakhand, this subterranean limestone cavern descends approximately 90 feet deep into the earth and extends 160 meters in length.

According to the Skanda Purana (Manaskhanda), it is believed that Lord Shiva resides here and that all 330 million (33 koti) deities of the Hindu pantheon have their divine presence sculpted naturally into the subterranean limestone stalactites and stalagmites. Entering the cave via heavy iron chains is like descending into the mystical womb of mother earth, surrounded by timeless natural rock architecture.

Mythology & Divine Rock Formations

Vedic legends recount that the cave was first discovered in Treta Yuga by King Rituparna of Ayodhya. Later in Dvapara Yuga, the Pandavas meditated here during their exile. In Kali Yuga, Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya re-established the sanctity of the cave in 1191 CE, consecrating the subterranean Shivling with eternal copper plates.

Inside the cave, visitors witness breathtaking natural calcite stalactite formations, including:

  • Sheshnag's Hood: Massive limestone rock formations resembling the cosmic multi-headed serpent holding the earth.
  • Ganesha's Severed Head: A smooth rock formation shaped like Lord Ganesha's head with a 108-petaled divine lotus above dripping sacred nectar.
  • The Four Yugas: Pillars representing Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali Yuga.
  • Kamdhenu & Kalpavriksha: Rock structures shaped like the wish-fulfilling divine cow and wish-granting celestial tree.
  • Kedarnath, Badrinath & Amarnath Models: Miniatures of the holy Himalayan shrines formed in stone.

How to Visit & Chain Descent Guide

Visiting Patal Bhuvaneshwar requires descending a narrow rock incline of about 75–80 feet. Two parallel iron chains are firmly anchored into the stone walls to support pilgrims on the way down and up. Comfortable cotton socks are recommended as footwear is taken off outside. Guides carry torches and the interior cavern is fully illuminated with electric lighting.
